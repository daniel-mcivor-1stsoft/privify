How to share login codes with your team without passing your phone around

November 2026 · 5 min read

Most business accounts want a mobile number for login codes: the bank, the card terminal, the courier portal, the wholesaler, the accounting software. Whoever set the account up usually used their own mobile.

That works until they're on holiday, off sick, asleep or no longer with you. Then someone needs to log in, the code goes to a phone in somebody else's pocket, and work stops while you track them down.

The usual workarounds, and why they fail

  • "Just text it to me." Every login depends on one person answering their phone, and codes end up scattered across personal chats.
  • A shared office phone in a drawer. Better, but it has to be charged, found and unlocked, and it doesn't help anyone working from home or on site.
  • Turning off two-step verification. It removes the problem by removing the protection. Don't.

Step 1: Check whether the service supports separate users

Many business accounts let you add colleagues with their own logins, each with their own two-step verification. Where that's available, use it. It's the most secure option, and it's how the service expects you to work.

Shared codes are for everything else: accounts that only allow one login, older portals, and accounts where only one person can be the main contact.

Step 2: Move shared accounts to a business number

Instead of one person's mobile, give each shared account a number that belongs to the business. When a code arrives, the people who need it can see it, wherever they are.

That number should be a genuine UK 07 mobile number, because some services won't send codes to landlines or internet numbers. It should be one you control, so it doesn't leave with an employee.

Step 3: Decide who can see the codes

Not everyone needs every code. Payroll and banking codes might be for the owner only, while courier and supplier codes can go to the whole team. Choose a setup where you decide who sees which number, and where you can take that away in one step when someone leaves.

Keep a list. Write down which accounts use the business number. When anything changes, such as a new number or someone leaving, you'll know exactly which logins to check.

How do I share a two-step verification code with my team?

Put the account on a shared business number instead of one person's mobile. Codes then arrive in a team inbox that the right people can see, so nobody has to forward codes from their own phone.

Is it safe to share login codes?

It's safer than sharing one person's phone, as long as only the people who need the codes can see them and you can remove someone's access when they leave. Where a service lets you add separate users, use that instead.

Why do login codes keep going to someone who has left?

The account was set up with that person's mobile number. Change the account's number to a business number that stays with the company, so codes keep reaching the team.
