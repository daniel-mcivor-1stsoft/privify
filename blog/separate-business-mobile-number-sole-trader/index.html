Should sole traders use a separate business mobile number?

November 2026 · 6 min read

Most sole traders start with the phone in their pocket. It's already paid for, customers can reach you straight away, and there's nothing to set up. For a while that works fine.

Then the number ends up on your van, your quotes, your website and a few hundred listings. Customers text at 10pm. A supplier's login codes go to the same phone as your family group chat. And if you ever take someone on, there's no way to give them access without giving them your phone.

A separate business number fixes most of that. Here's what the options are, what they cost, and which one suits a sole trader.

Why keep work and personal numbers apart?

  • Privacy. Your business number goes on adverts, invoices and directories. Your personal number stays with friends and family.
  • Switching off. You can choose when to check work messages instead of having them land between personal texts.
  • Looking established. One dedicated number on everything looks more professional than a personal mobile that changes when you change phone.
  • Growing. When you take someone on, they can use the business number too. With a personal mobile, that isn't possible.
  • Selling or closing. A business number can go with the business. Your personal number can't.

Option 1: A second phone or SIM

The simplest option is a second SIM, either in a second phone or as the second line on a dual-SIM handset. It gives you a real number with calls, texts and data.

The downside is that it's tied to one device. Only the person holding that phone sees the messages, and if you hand it to a colleague you've handed over everything on it. Running a second contract or SIM-only plan also adds a monthly cost, and a second handset if your phone isn't dual-SIM.

Option 2: A business VoIP or phone system

Internet-based phone systems give you a business number with call routing, menus and apps for each person. They're built for teams that take a lot of phone calls.

For a sole trader they're often more than you need, and many give you a landline-style or non-geographic number rather than an 07 mobile number. Some services also won't accept a VoIP number for text verification codes.

Option 3: A virtual mobile number

A virtual mobile number is a genuine UK 07 number that isn't tied to a SIM. Texts and voicemail arrive in an online inbox and by email, so you can read them on any device, and you can give other people access without handing over a phone.

It's built for receiving rather than calling. That suits a lot of sole traders: customers text or leave a voicemail, verification codes arrive reliably, and you reply when you're ready.

What should a sole trader look for?

  • A genuine 07 number, so customers recognise it and services accept it for codes.
  • Reliable login codes, because your bank, card terminal and supplier portals will send codes to it.
  • Voicemail with your own greeting, so missed callers hear your business name.
  • The option to add people later without changing the number.
  • VAT invoices, if you're VAT registered.

Frequently asked questions

Do sole traders need a separate business phone number?

There's no legal requirement, but most sole traders benefit from one. It keeps your personal number off invoices, adverts and listings, lets you switch off from work, and means the number can stay with the business if you take someone on or sell up.

What is the cheapest way to get a business mobile number?

A second SIM or a virtual mobile number is usually cheapest. A virtual number has no handset or SIM to manage and can be shared with other people, which a SIM can't.

Can I keep my existing number for the business?

Yes. You can usually move an existing UK mobile number to a new provider with a PAC code, so customers who already have it keep reaching you.
