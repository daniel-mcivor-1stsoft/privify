How to give your team one shared mobile number (without sharing a phone)

November 2026 · 6 min read

Most small businesses end up with one phone number that matters. It's on the website, the van, the invoices and every supplier account. Usually it's someone's personal mobile, and that someone becomes the bottleneck.

Here's how to give the whole team access to one business number without passing a phone around the office.

The problem with "the work phone"

The usual answers are a spare handset in a drawer, or the owner's own mobile. Both cause the same problems:

  • Login codes go to one person. Your bank, ad accounts and supplier portals text a code to whoever set them up. If they're on holiday, nobody can log in.
  • Messages get missed. A customer texts on Saturday, the phone is in a drawer, and nobody sees it until Monday.
  • Nobody knows who replied. Three people pick up the same phone and the customer gets three different answers.
  • The number leaves with the person. If it's a member of staff's own mobile, customers keep texting it after they've gone. We cover this in more detail in what happens to your business number when staff leave.
  • The owner is never off the clock. Work texts arrive on the same phone as family messages, at any hour.

What a shared number should do

The goal is simple: one number for the business that the right people can see, without anyone giving up their own mobile. In practice that means:

  • One inbox everyone can open, from any phone or computer.
  • Separate logins, so you can add and remove people without changing passwords.
  • Control over who sees what. Your bookkeeper needs the bank code but not every customer text.
  • A copy by email, so messages reach people who don't log in every day.
  • A number the business owns, which stays put when people come and go.

Your options in the UK

A spare SIM in a spare phone

Cheap to start, but only one person can hold it at a time. It runs out of battery, gets left in the van, and doesn't solve the login code problem.

A landline or VoIP phone system

Good for calls, but most services won't send login codes to landline numbers, and many VoIP systems don't receive texts at all.

WhatsApp Business on a shared phone

Useful for chatting with customers, but it's still tied to one phone number and handset, and it doesn't help with login codes from your other accounts.

A virtual mobile number with a team inbox

A genuine UK 07 number that lives online rather than in a handset. Texts, codes and voicemail arrive in a shared inbox that each team member opens with their own login. This is the option that covers everything in the list above.

Why a 07 number matters. Many services only send login codes to mobile numbers. A genuine UK 07 number receives them like any other mobile, so you can move your business accounts onto it.

How it works day to day

The login code

Your office manager needs to log in to the supplier portal while you're on a job. The code arrives in the team inbox and by email. They log in. Nobody phones you.

The customer text

A customer texts at 8am to move their appointment. Whoever is in first sees it and replies from the business number. Everyone else can see it's been handled.

The voicemail

Someone rings the business number after hours. They hear your own recorded greeting and leave a message. The audio and a written transcript arrive in the inbox and by email for the morning.

The new starter and the leaver

You add a new member of staff by email and choose what they can see. When someone leaves, you remove them. The number, the messages and the accounts stay with the business.

Setting it up

  1. Get the business number. A new 07 number, or move your existing one across with a PAC code.
  2. Move your shared accounts onto it. Bank, ad accounts, suppliers, marketplaces. Change the phone number in each account's security settings.
  3. Invite your team. Decide who shares the main number and who needs their own.
  4. Choose where copies go. Send texts and voicemail to the shared office email, the owner, accounts, or all three.
  5. Update your website and invoices with the new number, so your personal mobile can stop doing the job.
